# Incident response runbook (draft)

**Contacts and legal decisions must be completed before launch.**

1. Report suspected incident through the company-approved security channel; preserve time, reporter, affected systems, and correlation IDs.
2. Triage confidentiality, integrity, availability, scope, affected data/people, and active access. Escalate to security lead, management, HR and DPO.
3. Contain with authorized steps: disable compromised identities/sessions, revoke credentials, isolate affected service/storage, and preserve evidence. Avoid destructive cleanup before evidence capture.
4. Assess notification duties and deadlines with the DPO/legal owner using current official guidance. Do not let the application send legal breach notifications automatically.
5. Remediate, validate access, communicate through authorized channels, document decisions, and review lessons/actions.

The PDPC's *Guide on Managing and Notifying Data Breaches under the PDPA* states that, upon determining a breach is notifiable, the Commission must be notified as soon as practicable and no later than three calendar days; the first day is the day after determination. Whether a breach is notifiable and any affected-individual notice duties require authorized assessment. Source checked 2026-10-26: [PDPC guide (15 March 2021)](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Other-Guides/Guide-on-Managing-and-Notifying-Data-Breaches-under-the-PDPA-15-Mar-2021.pdf).

This is a process outline, not a legal determination. Add contacts, decision authority, provider escalation paths, evidence storage, and exercises before production.
